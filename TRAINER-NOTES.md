## How the slides were rewritten

From **How to Write Clearly**
- Short words, short sentences, one idea per line. "Adhesion" becomes "will it stick?"; "substrate" becomes "roof".
- Active voice: "Clean the roof", not "The roof should be cleaned".
- Every product number and spec from the original is kept exactly. Only the wording changed.

From **Made to Stick**
- **Simple:** each section opens with one *Remember* line (e.g. "Repair first, then restore").
- **Concrete:** job-site comparisons — primer on a car, rebar in concrete, a bedroom-sized square.
- **Unexpected:** "In the pull test, *failure* is the good result."
- **Credible:** real numbers — 90% of leaks start at details; the 25% wet rule.
- **Stories:** open with a roof you saved that the owner thought was dead.
- One core message repeated all day: **Clean. Dry. Sound.**

From **Workshop Survival Guide**
- Every block has a question for the crew. Let them answer before you do.
- Repeated slides (each coating system re-covers inspection, testing, prep and application) are marked
  **Review** — the crew teaches it back; the trainer fills gaps. This keeps a 251-slide deck moving.
- Hands-on moments: pass samples, fill out the authorization form in the room, save the tech rep's number.

## Respecting the room
- These are skilled roofers. Explain *product* words; never explain *roofing* to them.
- Call on the experienced people. Their stories carry more weight than a slide.
- No quizzes with names attached. Ask the room, not a person, until people are warmed up.

## Notes format
Each slide has up to four parts:
- **Key message** — the one thing to land.
- **Say it like this** — a plain-language talk track.
- **Make it stick** — an example or comparison.
- **Ask the crew** — a question to get them talking.

### 19. Silicone: The Strengths

*On slide:*
- Stays flexible from -15°F to 180°F
- Stretches without cracking. Sticks very well.
- Water can't soak through it
- Cures from moisture in the air — standing water won't soften it
- Holds up to sun — loses only about 0.1 mil a year
- 90%+ solids — more of what you spray stays on the roof
- Apply at 35°F and rising. Low fumes. Won't chalk.

**Key message:** Silicone stays put — through heat, cold, sun, and standing water.

**Say it like this:** High solids means when you put 10 gallons down, about 9 stay on the roof. It cures with moisture, so ponding water can't turn it back to goo.

**Make it stick:** Fun fact: silicon is the second most common element on Earth — about 27% of the earth's crust. It's basically made from sand.

**Ask the crew:** "Why do high solids matter to you as the installer?" (Less material, thicker film, fewer coats.)

### 20. Silicone: The Downsides

*On slide:*
- Picks up dirt more easily
- Only silicone sticks to silicone later
- Asphalt can stain through — needs a basecoat
- Harder to clean tools and spray rigs
- Needs commercial spray equipment
- Many crews haven't used it yet — newer tech (about 20 years)

**Key message:** Know the downsides so you can plan for them — not so you avoid silicone.

**Say it like this:** Be honest with customers about this. Most of these have a fix: our 988 resists dirt, the 294 basecoat stops asphalt stains, and training fixes unfamiliarity.

**Make it stick:** Silicone is a one-way street: once a roof is silicone, it stays silicone.

### 21. When Silicone Is a Good Fit

*On slide:*
- Flat roofs
- Low slopes — 3:12 or less
- Very hot or very cold places
- Max sun protection
- Areas where water sits*

*Footnote:* *Puddles, not lakes. The roof must still drain.

**Key message:** Flat, low-slope, harsh weather, ponding water: think silicone.

**Say it like this:** Ponding water is where silicone shines. But read the footnote: puddles, not lakes. The roof still has to drain.

### 22. When Silicone Is NOT a Good Fit

*On slide:*
- Deep standing water* — lakes on the roof
- Steep roofs — more than 3:12
- Barrel (curved) roofs
- Heavy foot traffic areas

*Footnote:* *Silicone handles puddles — not swimming pools.

**Key message:** Steep, curved, high-traffic, or swimming-pool roofs: pick something else.

**Say it like this:** Silicone runs on steep slopes and gets slick to walk on. On a roof with heavy foot traffic, use a tougher topcoat or add walk pads.

**Ask the crew:** "A roof holds 4 inches of water for a week after rain. Silicone?" (No — fix the drainage first.)

### 34. Step 2: Adhesion Test — Will It Stick?

*On slide:*
- Checks that the coating will stick to this roof
- Required on — single-ply, metal, smooth asphalt, and roofs coated before
- Best result — the coating tears before it lets go (called "cohesive failure")

**Key message:** Test a small patch first. If it doesn't stick, nothing else matters.

**Say it like this:** We put down a test patch and pull it. We want the coating to rip apart before it peels off the roof. That's called cohesive failure — and in this test, "failure" is the good result.

**Make it stick:** Like testing tape on a wall before you hang a picture. You don't hang the whole picture and hope.

**Ask the crew:** "Ever had a coating peel on you? What was the roof?"

### 35. How to Do the Pull Test

*On slide:*
- Cut — slice along both sides of the test strip
- Pull — lift it up and back, at 90° to 135°
- Measure — it must hold at least 2.0 lbs per inch
- Pass — the coating tears apart (cohesive)
- Fail — the coating peels off clean (adhesive)

**Key message:** Pass = the coating tears. Fail = it peels off clean.

**Say it like this:** Left photos: the coating peeled off the roof clean. That's a fail. Right: the coating ripped inside itself and stayed stuck to the roof. That's a pass. It also has to hold at least 2 pounds per inch.

**Make it stick:** If the coating comes off clean like a sticker, it will come off in the wind, too.

**Ask the crew:** Hold up a sample or point at the photo: "Pass or fail?"

### 36. Step 3: Moisture Survey — Is It Wet Inside?

*On slide:*
- Find wet insulation — infrared scans and core cuts
- More than 25% wet — not a candidate. Replace the roof.
- Less than 25% wet — cut out the wet areas and replace them
- Test methods — nuclear, infrared, impedance, or cores
- You need — a roof map and NRCA guidelines
- Not needed on metal roofs

**Key message:** The 25% rule: more than a quarter wet means it's not a restoration job.

**Say it like this:** You can't see wet insulation from the top. The scan finds it. If more than 25% of the roof is wet, we don't restore it. If it's less, we cut out the wet spots, replace them, and keep going.

**Make it stick:** Coating over wet insulation is like putting a new bandage over an infected cut. It looks fine — until it doesn't.

**Ask the crew:** "On a 100-square roof, how many squares can be wet and still be a candidate?" (Up to 25.)

### 73. Acrylic: The Strengths

*On slide:*
- Flexible and stretchy
- Sticks well
- Easy to apply — spray, brush, or roll
- Breathes — lets moisture vapor escape
- Other coatings can go over it later
- Good value. Low fumes.

**Key message:** Acrylic is easy, affordable, and flexible.

**Say it like this:** Easy to put down, easy to clean up, and you can coat over it later with almost anything.

### 74. Acrylic: The Downsides

*On slide:*
- Standing water softens it
- Needs 2 or more coats
- Weather matters — 50°F and rising, no rain for 24+ hours
- Can wash off if it rains too soon
- Takes longer to cure
- Mold and mildew can grow on it
- Loses about 1–2 mils a year to sun. Only about 50% solids.

*Footnote:* There are 380+ acrylic brands. Many use cheaper ingredients (styrene, vinyl).

**Key message:** Acrylic needs warm, dry weather and a roof that drains.

**Say it like this:** Water-based means rain is its enemy until it cures. Watch the forecast. And half of what's in the bucket evaporates — that's why it takes more coats.

**Make it stick:** Acrylic is like latex house paint. Great stuff — but you don't paint in the rain.

**Ask the crew:** "Forecast says rain in 18 hours. Do you spray acrylic today?" (No — it needs 24.)

### 75. When Acrylic Is a Good Fit

*On slide:*
- Steep roofs — 3:12 or more
- Metal roofs
- Flat roofs* — only in dry areas with great drainage
- Barrel (curved) roofs
- Hot, dry climates
- Little disruption. Budget-friendly.

*Footnote:* *Areas with little rain and good drainage — no ponding water.

**Key message:** Sloped, metal, curved, or dry climates: think acrylic.

**Say it like this:** Notice this is the opposite of silicone. Where silicone runs off, acrylic holds.

### 76. When Acrylic Is NOT a Good Fit

*On slide:*
- Ponding water
- Flat roofs* that don't drain

*Footnote:* *Little to no drainage.

**Key message:** Standing water and acrylic don't mix.

**Say it like this:** If water sits on the roof after a rain, acrylic will soften and fail. Pick silicone instead.

*On slide:*

### 135. Adhesion Test for Aluminum: The Tape Test

*On slide:*
- Different from the silicone and acrylic pull test
- Use for — coating over smooth asphalt, metal, or old coatings

- 2. Press clear tape firmly over the X

**Key message:** Aluminum uses a tape test, not a pull test. Coating on the tape means it fails.

**Say it like this:** This is the ASTM D3359 tape test. Cut an X, tape it, rip it back fast. Clean tape: pass. Coating on the tape: fail.
